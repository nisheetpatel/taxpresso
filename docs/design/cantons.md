# Several cantons: design note

Milestone 0.3.0 adds Zurich, Bern and Vaud. This note says how, with the least duplication. It started as a plan. Done so far: step 1, the federal split into `packages/rules-ch` with the canton interface types and the Geneva and Zurich adapters (section 1, "As built"); Zurich wired end to end as a preview (section 2, "As built: Zurich"); `packages/rules-be` and `packages/rules-vd`, Bern's and Vaud's cantonal and communal tax with their adapters, not wired yet.

## 1. What stays Geneva and what is Swiss

Today `packages/rules-ge` holds both the Geneva tax (ICC) and the federal tax (IFD), plus helpers every canton needs. The federal tax is the same in every canton; only the form it is declared on changes. Split it into a new package, `packages/rules-ch`, before wiring a second canton.

What moves to `packages/rules-ch` (same code, new home; `rules-ge` re-exports the moved names during the move so no import elsewhere changes at first):

| From `packages/rules-ge/src/` | To `packages/rules-ch/src/` | Why it is Swiss |
|---|---|---|
| `2025/icc.ts` (`Cited<T>` only), `engine/param.ts`, `engine/calc.ts` | `core/cited.ts`, `core/calc.ts` | Cited constants, integer centimes, rounding, `Trace` |
| `engine/types.ts` (`Amount`, `Line`, `Item`, `Text`, the household input) | `core/types.ts` | One input shape and one result shape for every canton (section 2) |
| `2025/ifd.ts`, `2026/ifd.ts`, `2026/ifd-amounts.ts` | `ifd/2025.ts`, `ifd/2026.ts` | Federal scales and deductions (ESTV circulars, Form. 58c) |
| the IFD half of `engine/assess.ts` and of `engine/compute.ts` (`personItems`) | `ifd/assess.ts`, `ifd/items.ts` | Taxable income to tax, art. 36 LIFD; federal deduction items |
| `engine/rules.ts` (flat professional costs, medical threshold, donations, two earners, 3a without a fund, meals) | `core/rules.ts` | Pure functions; each canton passes its own cited parameters |
| `2026/pillar3a.ts`, `2026/pension.ts` | `pension/` | OPP 3 and LPP limits, the 3a catch-up |
| `2026/withholding.ts`, `2026/exchange-rates.ts` | `withholding/` | Swiss withholding tax, DA-1, ESTV rates |
| `source-tax/ascii.ts` (the ESTV record format reader) | `source-tax/ascii.ts` | Federal file format; the Geneva tables stay in `data/ge/` |
| `engine/from-ledger.ts` | `core/from-ledger.ts` | Ledger facts to the household input; canton extras go in `input.cantonal` |
| `2026/keys-securities.ts`, `2026/keys-ech0196.ts`, the salary certificate part of `2026/document-keys.ts` | `keys/` | Federal form 11 boxes, eCH-0196: the same documents in every canton |

What stays in `rules-ge`: the ICC scales and amounts, splitting, the communal centimes and privileged shares, the LCP personal tax, the Geneva rubric codes, the état des titres (F2 to F4), TOU and the Geneva source-tax tables, the consistency checks that cite the Geneva guide, the assessment and objection deadline (LPFisc for the ICC; the LIFD half moves).

Federal items are keyed by concept, not by Geneva rubric: `rules-ch` returns `{ concept: 'professional.flat', person, amount, ... }` and each canton maps a concept to its form code (`31.20` in Geneva, `220` in the Bundessteuer column in Zurich).

Acceptance for the split: not one number changes. `packages/rules-ge/test/households.test.ts`, `ifd.test.ts`, `scales.test.ts` and the private golden cases pass unchanged, and `pnpm check:bundle` shows the same bundle contents.

### As built (step 1)

How the split was checked: every call of `computeReturn`, `assess` and `engineInputFromFacts` (Geneva) and of `computeReturn` and `assess` (Zurich) made by the whole test suite, plus a seeded fuzz of 4,000 Geneva and 2,500 Zurich households, was recorded before the split and after each step: all 15,532 results are byte-identical (items, lines, texts, sources, notes and limits in the same order). The tests pass with only import paths changed; `pnpm check:bundle` lists the same packages and built-ins, and the shipped files are the same (only workspace code moved); the built website is byte-identical; `scripts/source-tax-tables.mjs --check` and `scripts/zh-communes.mjs --check` still match the official files.

Where the table was too coarse, or the code asked for another boundary:

- `engine/from-ledger.ts` stays in `rules-ge` for now. It reads Geneva's commune list and Geneva's état des titres (`etat-des-titres.ts`, which stays); it moves with step 2, when it gives a `HouseholdInput` for any canton with Geneva's extras under `cantonal`.
- `2026/ifd.ts` and `2026/ifd-amounts.ts` stay two files, `ifd/2026.ts` and `ifd/2026-amounts.ts`: the website imports `ifd/2026.ts` one module at a time with Node's type stripping, which cannot follow a value import, and the amounts import the 3a limits. New: `ifd/params.ts` (`ifdParamsFor(year)`, the federal constants of a year, which Geneva's `paramsFor` now takes as its `ifd`).
- `2026/pillar3a.ts` is `pension/pillar3a.ts`; `2026/pension.ts` is `pension/buy-ins.ts` (LPP buy-ins and the 3a catch-up).
- `withholding/withholding.ts` has its own exact rounding to the franc (BigInt, safe integers only), different from `core/calc.ts`'s `roundFranc`: `rules-ch` exports it as `roundFrancExact`, `rules-ge` still exports it as `roundFranc`.
- `engine/rules.ts`: the six shared rules moved to `core/rules.ts`; `lifeInsuranceCap` (ICC 52.15) and `avsDeduction` (ICC 61.30) are Geneva's and stay. Zurich's `mealsDeduction` and `medicalDeduction` were the same code and now come from `rules-ch`.
- The IFD half of `engine/compute.ts` is `ifd/items.ts`: one function per federal deduction (pillar 3a, professional costs, travel and its pricing, training, two earners, insurance, debt interest, childcare, the couple and child deductions, donations, parties) and `federalScale` (the scale and the reductions per child, circular 30). Each returns a deduction keyed by concept (`FederalConcept`) with its trace, the limits it applied and its notes; Geneva calls each at the same place as before and puts it on its rubric (`RUBRIC_OF` in `compute.ts`). Not one function that computes every federal item at once: Geneva interleaves ICC and IFD items, and the order of the result's notes and limits would change. Meals and medical costs have the same rule in both taxes, so they are shared helpers (`core/items.ts`, `mealsCosts`, `medicalCosts`) that Geneva's ICC uses too. The base of the medical and donation shares is named by the canton (`FormBase`: Geneva's subtotals 92.20 and 92.40).
- The IFD half of `engine/assess.ts` is `ifd/assess.ts` (`ifdTax`), from the taxable income on. The subtotals (91.00 to 99.00) follow the canton's form and stay in `rules-ge` (`incomeTotals`).
- The LIFD half of the objection deadline is `ifd/objection.ts` (`IFD_OBJECTION`); Geneva's half moved to `rules-ge/src/2026/objection.ts`, so the website still imports both one module at a time.
- Types (`core/types.ts`): the shared shapes, `IfdTax` and `IfdResult` (the federal part; Geneva's adds 92.20, 92.40 and 95.00), `CantonalResult` and `TaxResult` (the shared top level, typed by the canton's `icc`). `Param` takes the estimate reason as a type parameter (Geneva: `'ge-2026-unpublished'`). `HouseholdInput` is the union of the facts both engines read, each field saying which canton reads it, with `cantonal` for what only one canton asks for. Geneva's `EngineInput` keeps its shape: the household input without Zurich's facts, plus `splitting` and `wealthItems`. Zurich's `ZhEngineInput` is unchanged.
- The salary certificate keys are `keys/salary-certificate.ts` (`KEYS_SALARY_CERTIFICATE_2026` and the field list `SALARY_CERTIFICATE_FIELDS_2026`); Geneva's `DOCUMENT_KEYS_2026` spreads them first, same keys in the same order.
- `rules-zh` keeps `src/calc.ts` as a re-export of `rules-ch`'s helpers (its exports and one test import it); `src/cited.ts` is gone.
- The federal tests stay where they were (`rules-ge/test/ifd.test.ts`, `scales.test.ts`, `rules.test.ts`, `withholding.test.ts`, `source-tax-ascii.test.ts`), importing from `rules-ch`. `rules-ch` has its own citation test (`test/sources.test.ts`: every constant, fact and key source, and the citations written into the federal rules, are `ok` in `docs/sources/lock.json`). Moving the federal tests next to their code is a follow-up.
- Workspace: nothing to add. `pnpm-workspace.yaml` (`packages/*`), `vitest.config.ts`, ESLint and Prettier cover every package by glob, and there is no root `tsconfig.json` with references; each package has its own `tsconfig.json`. `extension/allowed-dependencies.json` is unchanged: workspace code is bundled, not an npm package.

## 2. The canton interface

Each canton is three packages and one data folder, the shape Geneva already has:

```
packages/rules-<c>/      the cantonal and communal tax, by year; exports `rules: CantonRules`
packages/filing-<c>/     form map, copy panel order, reconciliation; exports `filing: CantonFiling`
data/<c>/<year>/         questionnaire.yaml (and canton data files)
docs/sources/<c>/        manifest.json, NOTES.md (already there for 15 cantons)
```

The interface lives in `packages/rules-ch/src/canton.ts` (types only):

```ts
export interface CantonRules {
  canton: 'GE' | 'ZH' | 'BE' | 'VD';
  taxYears: readonly number[];                     // years with official values; others throw
  communes(taxYear: number): readonly Commune[];    // { bfs, slug, name, rates }, cited
  computeReturn(input: HouseholdInput): TaxResult;  // cantonal + communal + IFD (via rules-ch)
  keys(taxYear: number): KeyRegistry;               // rules-ch shared keys + the canton's own
  documents(taxYear: number): readonly DocumentRule[]; // what to attach, with the guide page
  sourcesIndex: string;                             // docs/sources/<c>, for search_official_docs
}

export interface CantonFiling {
  formMap(taxYear: number): FormMap;                // pages, sections, codes, labels as printed
  formValues(result: TaxResult, facts: LedgerView): FormValues;  // copy panel
  reconcile(pdfText: string, values: FormValues): Reconciliation;
}
```

- `HouseholdInput` is Geneva's `EngineInput` with the Geneva-only fields moved under `cantonal` (Geneva: `splitting`; Zurich: `church`, `communeVariant`; Vaud: the quotient familial parts). `packages/rules-zh/src/types.ts` already follows `EngineInput`'s field names, so `from-ledger` serves both.
- `TaxResult` keeps today's top level (`taxYear`, `commune`, `icc`, `ifd`, `total`, `estimates`, `needsOk`, `complete`, `incomplete`, `assumptions`, `notCounted`, `unsupported`, `unverifiedRules`). `icc` means "cantonal and communal tax" in every canton; its common fields (`items`, `grossIncome`, `netIncome`, `taxableIncome`, `wealth.items`, `wealth.taxableWealth`, `taxes.total`) are what the dashboard reads, and `taxes` carries the canton's own lines (Geneva: centimes additionnels, 12 % reduction; Zurich: einfache Staatssteuer, Steuerfuss, Personalsteuer). The dashboard then shows `taxes` as an ordered list of lines and needs no canton code.
- Communes are identified by their BFS number (federal commune number), which every canton's tables carry; `slug` stays for display and for Geneva's facts already stored as slugs (`profile.commune`).
- The questionnaire engine already takes its file, keys and communes as arguments (`new QuestionnaireEngine(file, keys, communes)`): beyond asking `profile.canton` first, only its "is not a Geneva commune" message names a canton.
- The extension gets one registry, `extension/src/cantons.ts`: `{ GE: { rules, filing, questionnaire }, ZH: ... }`. `main.ts` stops importing `COMMUNES_2026` and `KEYS_2026` from `rules-ge` and asks the registry for the canton of the year being prepared.
- Prior-return parsing (`packages/intake/src/prior/`) and the état des titres are form-specific: they move behind `CantonFiling` when a second canton needs them; until then Zurich has none.

### As built (step 1)

`packages/rules-ch/src/canton.ts` has the types; `rules-ge` and `rules-zh` each export `rules`, an adapter tested to give exactly what the engine gives (`test/canton.test.ts` in each: about 200 households each, through the interface and directly). Differences from the sketch above:

- `CantonRules<Cantonal, Result>` takes the canton's `cantonal` part and its own result as type parameters; with the defaults it is the interface above, and a canton's `rules` is assignable to it. `CantonCode` names the cantons with a rules package (Fribourg, `FR`, added with `packages/rules-fr`; Ticino, `TI`, with `packages/rules-ti`; Aargau, `AG`, with `packages/rules-ag`; Zug, `ZG`, with `packages/rules-zg`).
- `Commune` is `{ bfs, slug, name, rates }` with `bfs: number | null` and `rates: Cited<readonly number[]>` (Geneva: the centimes additionnels in hundredths; Zurich: the Steuerfuss without church tax, one per part of the commune). Geneva's communes have `bfs: null`: no document in the library gives Geneva's federal commune numbers (the guide prints a "code lieu" per commune, p. 59, without calling it that). Zurich's slug is its folded name.
- `DocumentRule` is `{ kind, keys }`: what a kind of document gives, the schema Claude reads it against (today's `documentFields`). What to attach, with the guide's page, stays in the questionnaire's requirements (`data/ge/2026/questionnaire.yaml`), which already cite the guide.
- `CantonFiling<FormMap, FormValues, Reconciliation>` takes the filing package's shapes; `LedgerView` is the current facts and documents. Nothing implements it yet.
- Geneva's adapter puts `splitting` and `wealthItems` under `cantonal`; Zurich's puts `communeVariant`, `marriedTariff` and `church` there. Each adapter refuses a fact that would change its tax but that its engine does not read, rather than drop it: Geneva refuses `unemploymentBenefits` (Geneva counts them in `replacementIncome`), a side job, a child who does not live with you, Zurich's household amounts (child support paid or received, other income, cash, life insurance value, vehicles) and a commune given by BFS number; Zurich refuses a 3a catch-up, childcare for the household, accounts owned by a child and a commune given by name. Zurich leaves aside the facts only Geneva's rules use (age band, Geneva's meal rule, the commune of work, the withholding tax credit shown next to the tax).
- `keys(taxYear)`: Geneva gives `KEYS_2026` for both its years; Zurich gives the shared keys only (salary certificate, securities, eCH-0196) until it has a questionnaire.
- Zurich's `computeReturn` gave `ifd: null` at step 1; it now gives the federal tax of its Bundessteuer column (see "As built: Zurich" below).

### As built: Zurich (the canton question, its questions, estimate, filing and German)

What a Zurich resident can do now, as for Geneva: answer the questions, see the estimate (cantonal, communal, church on request, personal tax, and the federal tax), and get the values to type in the Zurich form's order. How it is built, and what adding the next canton (Bern) touches:

- **Registry** (`extension/src/cantons.ts`): one `CantonEntry` per canton: code, names (EN, FR, DE), its free software (GeTax, ZHprivateTax), its `rules`, its questionnaire, keys and communes for a year, and how to tell its earlier answers (Geneva: a Geneva commune, a move within Geneva, TOU). `CantonRegistry` gives the canton of the year (`state`: the answer, earlier answers, or none), the ledger's keys (every canton's, merged) and one questionnaire engine per canton. `SUPPORTED_CANTONS` (`packages/rules-ch/src/keys/canton.ts`) lists the codes the `profile.canton` question offers; adding Bern there and in the registry, plus `data/be/<year>/questionnaire.yaml`, gives Bern its questions.
- **Questions** (`data/ch/2026/questions.yaml`): the questions every canton shares, in EN, FR and DE, with the federal Form 2a as `learnMore`. A canton's questionnaire takes them with `- use: <id>` and overrides what differs (its guide's page, its own options); `data/zh/2026/questionnaire.yaml` adds the commune (with the parts of communes whose school districts differ), the move, the church per person and the parish. Shared keys moved to `packages/rules-ch/src/keys/` (household, attestations, wealth); `rules-ge` composes them in the same order (its keys are byte-identical).
- **Reading the ledger**: `packages/rules-ch/src/core/from-ledger.ts` (`householdFromFacts`) reads what every canton shares; a canton gives its `CantonReading` (its communes, its form's lines for the notes). Geneva adds its état des titres; Zurich (`packages/rules-zh/src/from-ledger.ts`) adds the church, the parish, the married tariff in shared custody, and the movable wealth read with the shared securities rules (`extension/src/zurich-wealth.ts`).
- **Federal tax on a canton's form**: `packages/rules-zh/src/ifd.ts` puts each `FederalConcept` on its Zurich line (220, 260, 270, 365...), with the Zurich form's own order where it differs from Geneva's (the married couple deduction after 350, donations and party payments on the Nettoeinkommen 310). A seeded test (2,000 households) shows the federal tax is the same as Geneva's for the same household, and pins the documented differences.
- **Estimate, final check, plans**: `Workspace.estimate()` runs the canton's reading and engine; `extension/src/estimate-zh.ts` words Zurich's result for compute_tax and the dashboard. The final check (`rules-ge`, `finalCheck`) takes a canton's words (`FinalCheckCanton`: its labels, what was given per rule, its software, its documents for the links; Zurich's in `packages/rules-zh/src/final-check.ts`). The plans (`packages/optimizer`) run on Zurich's engine (`zurichEstimator`); TOU stays Geneva's.
- **Filing** (`packages/filing-zh`): the form map from the official 2025 return and annexes, and the values to type from Zurich's engine; the copy panel itself (`filing-ge`, `buildCopyPanel`) takes any canton's page ids and column headings. The check of the official PDF stays Geneva's and says so.
- **German**: the dashboard has German strings for every card; the questions, canton names, document kinds and Zurich's estimate are German on a German page; the other views' content is English there for now (`extension/src/lang.ts`).

Adding Bern today: the registry entry and `SUPPORTED_CANTONS`, `data/be/<year>/questionnaire.yaml` and Bern's keys, a `CantonReading` and a branch in `Workspace.estimate()`, a view of its result like `estimate-zh.ts`, its final check words, `packages/filing-be`, and the planner's estimator. The estimate view, the final check words and the estimator are the parts a generic `TaxResult` view (the dashboard showing `taxes` as an ordered list of lines, section 2) would remove; that refactor is the next step before Bern.

## 3. Choosing the canton

- The canton is the one you lived in on 31 December of the tax year. That canton taxes the whole year, cantonal, communal and federal tax, when you moved from another canton during the year (zh-2025-guide-natuerliche-personen p. 5 and 6; ge-2025-guide-declaration-pp p. 53). The rates are those in force at the end of the year (Zurich: StG § 53, zh-2026-law-steuergesetz-631-1-stand-2026 p. 27).
- The commune follows the same rule inside Zurich: the commune you lived in at the end of the year taxes the whole year (StG § 190, zh-2026-law-steuergesetz-631-1-stand-2026 p. 73).
- New first question, `profile.canton` (stated, `via: chat | dashboard`), asked before the commune. It is prefilled from last year's return when there is one, never guessed from an address in a document.
- A move between cantons during the year then stops being "not supported" (today `data/ge/2026/questionnaire.yaml`, question `moved`, option `from_other_canton`): the new canton's return covers the whole year. Still out of scope: arriving from or leaving for abroad (part-year liability), and property or a business in another canton (intercantonal allocation).
- A canton Taxpresso does not cover yet: the canton question says so and stops, like any other "not supported" answer.

## 4. Vault layout

No per-canton folder. One tax year has one canton, so `~/Documents/Taxpresso/<year>/` stays as it is and the canton is a fact in that year's ledger (`profile.canton`). Documents are canton-neutral (salary certificates and bank statements are the same everywhere). A household that moves has `2025/` with Geneva and `2026/` with Zurich, each folder saying which canton it is. If the user corrects the canton, the facts stay; answers to questions the new canton doesn't ask are kept but not used, and say so. Last year's review compares across cantons only what is federal.

## 5. What each new canton needs

1. Sources: the consolidated tax law for each year, the tariff (or cold-progression ordinance), the cantonal multiplier decision, the communal multipliers table, the guide and forms, the professional-costs ordinance; fetched, conflicts in `docs/sources/<c>/NOTES.md`.
2. `packages/rules-<c>`: `src/<year>/` cited constants that throw for an unknown year; `src/<year>/communes.ts` generated from the official table by `scripts/<c>-communes.mjs` (with `--check`); `computeReturn` to `TaxResult`; tests on the official worked examples, 4 to 6 synthetic households with hand derivations, and a test that every citation is an `ok` entry of `docs/sources/lock.json`.
3. `data/<c>/<year>/questionnaire.yaml` and `src/<year>/keys.ts`, with the test that keeps them in step (as for Geneva).
4. `packages/filing-<c>`: the form map from the official specimen (codes, labels, pages, cited), the copy panel order, the reconciliation parser for the canton's official PDF.
5. Extension: register the canton in `extension/src/cantons.ts`; server instructions and the "not supported" texts name the canton's own free software (ZHprivateTax, TaxMe-Online in Bern, VaudTax); bundle the canton's source passages (SPEC.md 7b).
6. UI: German strings for Zurich and Bern (SPEC.md section 1 already plans German); official terms in the canton's language next to the gloss.
7. [HUMAN] Golden households entered in the canton's official software, matched to the franc; the canton's field-map capture.

## 6. Order of work

1. `packages/rules-ch` split (section 1), Geneva unchanged to the centime. Done, with the `CantonRules` types and the Geneva and Zurich adapters.
2. `from-ledger` for any canton (`HouseholdInput`), the registry with Geneva only, and `profile.canton`.

4. Bern: reuses the German UI and the Zurich work on German forms. Started: `packages/rules-be`, Bern's cantonal and communal tax on `rules-ch`, with its adapter `rules` (not wired).
5. Vaud: reuses the French UI. Started: `packages/rules-vd`, the cantonal and communal income and wealth tax for 2025 and 2026 (not wired), its communes generated from the communal tables (.xls) by `scripts/vd-communes.mjs`; Vaud's communes have no BFS number in the official tables, so `Commune.bfs` and the result's commune `bfs` are null (rules-ch's `TaxResult` allows it). Open points and conflicts: `docs/sources/vd/NOTES.md`, "Open points for the rules".
6. Basel-Stadt (not in milestone 0.3.0): started, `packages/rules-bs`, the cantonal and communal income and wealth tax for 2025 and 2026 (not wired; `CantonCode` gained `'BS'`), the shares and multipliers of Basel, Riehen and Bettingen generated from the canton's table of rates by `scripts/bs-communes.mjs`; no BFS numbers in the documents, so `bfs` is null as for Vaud. Its engine asks for facts the other cantons don't (each person's work rate and reference age, cohabiting for single parents, who mainly bears a shared child's upkeep, whether each child is under 18, the gross income of every account), under `cantonal`. Open points and conflicts: `docs/sources/bs/NOTES.md`, "Open points for the rules".
7. Fribourg: reuses the French UI. Started: `packages/rules-fr`, the cantonal, communal and opt-in church income and wealth tax (not wired). Tax year 2025 is computed; 2026 has every value but the communal coefficients, which no document gives yet, so its result says so instead of computing. Communes carry their BFS numbers (the open data tables give them), generated with their parishes by `scripts/fr-communes.mjs`; `scripts/fr-tariff.mjs --check` compares the income scale with all 4,091 rows of the SCC's detailed tariff. Open points and conflicts: `docs/sources/fr/NOTES.md`, "Open points for the rules".
8. Ticino (not in milestone 0.3.0): started, `packages/rules-ti`, the cantonal and communal income and wealth tax and the communal personal tax for 2025 and 2026 (not wired), on the lines (cifre) of the main return, Modulo 1, which the canton publishes only as a German translation. The tariffs are the law's (LT art. 35 as each year's executive decree rewrote it, art. 49), kept to every amount the law prints and compared with all 235 amounts of the canton's 2025 income table (within 10 centimes below CHF 380,600 and 50 above: the canton's method between the law's amounts is not published) and the 122 of its wealth table (equal); the communal multipliers of 99 communes in 2025 and 100 in 2026 are generated from the canton's table by `scripts/ti-communes.mjs` (no BFS numbers, so `bfs` is null). The return has no federal column (the authority derives the federal tax), so `ifd` is null. Its engine asks, under `cantonal`, for each person's work rate and months of work (the flat professional costs), whether each child is under 18, and whether a divorce, separation or death happened in the year. Open points and conflicts: `docs/sources/ti/NOTES.md`, "Open points for the rules".
9. Basel-Landschaft: reuses the German UI. Started: `packages/rules-bl`, the state, communal and opt-in church income and wealth tax for 2025 and 2026 (not wired; `CantonCode` gained `'BL'`). The income tariff is the formula of StG § 34 with each year's parameters from the canton's tariff workbook; `scripts/bl-tariff.mjs --check` compares them with the workbook and the formula with every row of its table. The 86 communes' Steuerfüsse (and the 2026 church rates) are generated from the canton's table of rates by `scripts/bl-communes.mjs`; no BFS numbers in the documents, so `bfs` is null. The child deduction is a reduction of the tax (CHF 750), insurance premiums a flat maximum, illness costs and donations deductible in full. The church tax is computed for the Roman Catholic church in 2026 only. Its engine asks, under `cantonal`, each pensioner's kind of pension (AHV or IV, 2026), whether each child has taxable income of its own, who mainly bears a shared child's upkeep, and each adult's church. Open points and conflicts: `docs/sources/bl/NOTES.md`, "Open points for the rules".
10. Aargau: reuses the German UI. Started: `packages/rules-ag`, the cantonal, communal and opt-in church income and wealth tax for 2025 and 2026 (not wired; `CantonCode` gained `'AG'`). The tariffs are the law's (StG § 43 with the steps of the Progressionsverordnung 2025, § 55), Tarif B the rate of half the income; `scripts/ag-tariff.mjs --check` compares them with every row of the canton's tables (3,701 per Tarif, 4,200 for wealth), both valid from 2025, the newest the canton lists. The communal and church Steuerfüsse of 197 communes in 2025 and 196 in 2026, with each commune's further parishes (Zusatzkirchen), are generated from the canton's lists by `scripts/ag-communes.mjs`; no BFS numbers in the documents, so `bfs` is null. The insurance deduction is a flat amount whatever was paid (the adapter leaves the premiums aside); the taxes are rounded to 10 centimes as the guide's worked example does, a rule no document states. The main form is not published, so the lines carry the guide's section numbers (1.1 to 34); the federal part of the return is not mapped, so `ifd` is null. ## Open points for the lead

- The Zurich communal table gives two or three multipliers for some communes (Uster and Turbenthal in 2025 and 2026: two school districts) without naming the parts. `rules-zh` asks for the variant and does not compute without it. The questionnaire needs the area names from the commune or the canton.
- Zurich's rounding of taxable amounts and of the tax is not in the documents in the library (`docs/sources/zh/NOTES.md`, "Open points for the rules"); `rules-zh` reports what it assumed in `unverifiedRules`. One official tax example (the online calculator's printout or an assessment) would settle it.
- The 2026 Staatssteuerfuss: the Kantonsrat decision says 95 %, the 2026 source-tax parameter sheet says 98 % (see `docs/sources/zh/NOTES.md`). `rules-zh` uses the decision and shows the conflict to the user, on every 2026 Zurich estimate, as a rule the documents don't spell out.
- Some household input fields mean slightly different things in the two engines: `replacementIncome` holds unemployment benefits in Geneva (11.50) but not in Zurich, where the shared reading puts them on `unemploymentBenefits` (140/141, `CantonReading.unemploymentApart`). Child support paid is part of Geneva's `alimonyPaid` (53.10) but its own field in Zurich (`childSupportPaid`, 255); no ledger key gives alimony or child support amounts yet, so neither canton counts them and both say so.
- Geneva's federal commune numbers: a document of the library that gives them would let Geneva's communes carry `bfs` like Zurich's.
- A generic view of a canton's result (section 2: `taxes` as an ordered list of lines) would let Bern and Vaud reuse the estimate card, compute_tax, the final check words and the planner's estimator instead of a module each, as Zurich has now.
- Zurich's securities: Taxpresso reads them with the shared securities rules and puts their totals on 150 and 400; the Zurich securities list itself (Wertschriftenverzeichnis, with the withholding tax refund) is not laid out yet, and the securities card still shows Geneva's état des titres.
- The MCP prompt keeps its id `start_geneva_tax_return` (so nothing that calls it breaks); its title and text are canton-neutral now.
